Bathroom Water Damage: What Cranston Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. Our Cranston team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

What It Looks Like

  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • The ceiling beneath the bathroom shows a brownish ring or has started to droop
  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Leaking Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Rot Under the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.
